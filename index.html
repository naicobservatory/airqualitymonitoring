How They Affect Your Health

PM10 Health Effects

Deposition

Deposits in upper airway and throat

Symptoms

Coughing, wheezing, throat irritation

Risk Level

Moderate — primarily respiratory

PM2.5 Health Effects

Deposition

Penetrates deep into lungs and bloodstream

Symptoms

Asthma, bronchitis, cardiovascular issues

Risk Level

High — systemic and respiratory effects

Understanding DENR EMB AQI Levels

Sources: DAO 2020-14 (PM2.5), DAO 2013-13 (PM10), DENR EMB Procedural Manual on Data Handling Protocols 2021.

DENR EMB 24-Hour Averaging: All PM2.5 and PM10 concentrations are averaged over the past 24 hours before computing the AQI. This aligns with official DENR EMB reporting standards under the Philippine Clean Air Act (RA 8749).

How Overall AQI is Calculated: The DENR AQI is computed separately for each pollutant via linear interpolation of the raw concentration against the breakpoint table. The highest individual sub-index determines the overall reported AQI (the "maximum operator" method per DENR EMB Procedural Manual MC 2021-07).

⚠ Note on AQI Scale: The DENR EMB AQI uses a 0–91+ concentration-based scale — not the US EPA 0–500 scale. A DENR AQI of 25 means "Good," while a value above 91 is "Emergency." This is a distinct Philippine standard and values cannot be directly compared to US AQI numbers.
